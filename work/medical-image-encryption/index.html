Encrypting colour medical images with chaos

A 3D hyperchaotic map, an S-box and a discrete wavelet transform for secure colour medical image encryption. Published at ICIMTH 2025.

Reehab AhmadCollaborative research · published in Stud Health Technol InformFeb 20251 min read

Problem

Medical images travel between hospitals, clouds and devices constantly, and they are sensitive. Standard encryption schemes treat images as generic data, but images have strong correlations between neighbouring pixels that an encryption scheme has to destroy thoroughly.

Approach

The method uses a 3D hyperchaotic map to drive permutation (shuffling pixel positions) and diffusion (changing pixel values, strengthened with an S-box), followed by a discrete wavelet transform. Chaotic systems are extremely sensitive to initial conditions, which gives a very large key space.

Result

The scheme achieved a UACI of 36.17% and an NPCR of 99.57%, the standard measures of how strongly a small change in the input image changes the cipher image, with a large key space.

Published as: Abbasi SF, Ahmad R, Mukherjee T, Ding X, Bai L, Pournik O, Arvanitis TN. A Novel and Secure 3D Colour Medical Image Encryption Technique Using 3D Hyperchaotic Map, S-box and Discrete Wavelet Transform. Studies in Health Technology and Informatics. 2025;328:268–272. Presented at ICIMTH 2025.
