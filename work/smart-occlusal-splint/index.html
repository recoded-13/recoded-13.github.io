A mouthguard that notices you grinding

A wearable intraoral splint with a piezoelectric sensor that detects bruxism episodes from bite force, instead of only protecting against them.

Reehab AhmadLead research project · Riphah International UniversityAug 20241 min read

Problem

Bruxism, the clenching and grinding of teeth, is closely associated with psychological stress, and much of it happens during sleep. The standard treatment is an occlusal splint: a hard guard that protects the teeth. It is purely passive. It absorbs the damage but records nothing: not when episodes happen, how often, or how hard. Patients and clinicians have little objective data to work with.

Approach

If the splint is already in the mouth, it is in the perfect place to measure bite force. The idea is to embed a force sensor in a custom occlusal splint and turn a protective device into a measuring one. With a record of when, how often and how hard, you can eventually add feedback that interrupts an episode as it starts.

What I built

  • Sensor integration. A BHF350-3AA piezoelectric sensor embedded in a custom-made occlusal splint.
  • Acquisition. The sensor interfaced with an STM32 microcontroller for analog signal acquisition.
  • Characterisation. Systematic testing of the sensor’s response inside the splint, so later detection thresholds rest on measured behaviour and not guesses.

Result

Sensor characterisation is complete. Work in progress covers noise filtering, real-time episode detection, and a feedback mechanism.

What I learned

Putting electronics in a mouth is a lesson in constraints: space, moisture, comfort and safety all compete with signal quality. It also reframed the project for me. The interesting problem isn’t the sensor. It’s deciding which pattern of force counts as an episode, and what a useful response would be.
